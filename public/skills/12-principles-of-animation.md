# 12 Principles of Animation
**Author:** raphaelsalaja
**Domain:** Motion & Interaction
**Purpose:** Apply Disney's 12 classic animation principles to web interfaces — squash & stretch, anticipation, follow-through, and more.

## What It Does
Translates the foundational Disney animation principles (from The Illusion of Life) to web UI context. Evaluates whether interface animations follow the physical and psychological rules that make motion feel natural and believable.

## The 12 Principles Applied to Web
1. Squash & Stretch — elements deform slightly under physics
2. Anticipation — small preparation before main action
3. Staging — clear composition directing attention
4. Straight Ahead & Pose to Pose — keyframe vs continuous
5. Follow Through & Overlapping — parts finish at different times
6. Slow In & Slow Out — easing, not linear motion
7. Arc — curved natural paths of motion
8. Secondary Action — supporting details reinforce main action
9. Timing — duration matches weight and energy
10. Exaggeration — slightly beyond reality for clarity
11. Solid Drawing — 3D awareness in 2D space
12. Appeal — engaging, clear, and readable motion

## Input Requirements
- Animation or interaction to evaluate
- Target element type and context
- Intended emotional feel

## Output
- Principle-by-principle evaluation
- Violations with specific fixes
- Easing and timing recommendations

## Quality Criteria
Natural motion follows physics. Exaggeration serves clarity, not performance. Timing must match the perceived weight of the element.

## Edge Cases
- Principles that conflict in a single animation
- Micro-interactions too small for full principle application
- Principles that add latency in high-frequency interactions
- Platform constraints that prevent arc-based motion

## Best Practices
- Slow In/Slow Out (easing) is the minimum applied to all motion
- Anticipation on important CTAs increases perceived responsiveness
- Follow-through on dismissals makes exits feel complete
- Timing: heavy elements move slower, light elements faster
- Exaggeration: 10–15% beyond reality is usually sufficient
